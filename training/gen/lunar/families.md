# Lunar and lunisolar holidays (round of 2026-11-21)

Goal: phrases a person types or writes that name one of these holidays as a date or a period. This file lists the kinds of phrasing we already expect. It shows no output formats. We want to find kinds we have not thought of, so unusual real phrasings matter more than many copies of the common ones.

## Holidays in scope

| Calendar | Holidays |
|---|---|
| Chinese | Lunar New Year (Chinese New Year, Spring Festival, Tet, Seollal), Mid-Autumn Festival (Moon Festival, Mooncake Festival, Chuseok), Dragon Boat Festival (Duanwu, Tuen Ng) |
| Hindu | Diwali (Deepavali), Holi |
| Islamic | Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha (Bakrid), Ramadan |
| Hebrew | Rosh Hashanah, Yom Kippur, Passover (Pesach), Hanukkah (Chanukah), Purim |

Cover every holiday. Each should get at least 5% of your phrases.

## Families

| Code | Family | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| A | The name alone or with a plain joiner | "diwali", "on eid", "for passover", "chinese new year", "yom kippur" |
| B | A relative modifier | "next diwali", "this ramadan", "last hanukkah", "the coming eid", "this year's rosh hashanah" |
| C | A year | "diwali 2027", "ramadan 2028", "lunar new year 2030", "passover of 2029" |
| D | A time or part of the day | "diwali at 7pm", "eid morning", "yom kippur evening", "cny dinner 6:30" |
| E | Eves, nights, first and last days, days before and after | "erev yom kippur", "first night of hanukkah", "the day after eid", "chinese new year's eve", "2nd day of rosh hashanah", "last day of ramadan", "end of ramadan", "3 days before diwali" |
| F | A holiday as a period | "during ramadan", "over hanukkah", "all of passover", "the 8 days of hanukkah", "throughout ramadan" |
| G | Ranges and bounds with other dates or holidays | "from diwali to new year", "until eid", "between rosh hashanah and yom kippur", "after passover", "starting ramadan", "eid to the following monday" |
| H | Inside a sentence or message | "office closed for eid al adha, back the day after", "can we do the call after yom kippur?", "flying home for cny" |
| I | Other spellings, transliterations, regional names | "deepavali", "chanukah", "pesach", "ramzan", "bakrid", "hari raya", "tet", "seollal", "chuseok", "spring festival", "duanwu", "tuen ng", "eid ul fitr", "rosh hashana" |
| J | Typos, abbreviations, voice, odd spacing | "diwaly", "hannukah", "yom kipur", "eid-ul-adha", "cny", "rosh hashana nite", "ramadhan" |
| K | Near misses and negatives | phrases that look similar but name no date, or a different one: "eid mubarak", "diwali sale", "holi colors", "passover recipes", "every diwali", "ramadan kareem", "holiday", "the holidays" |
| L | Anything else a person might write with these meanings | constructions that fit none of the above: write them and add a short note |

## Variation to cover

- Context words around the phrase: "remind me", "book", "out of office", "deadline", "party", "dinner", "trip", "family visit", "school closed", event names, filler.
- Registers: terse quick-add, voice transcript (numbers as words, no punctuation), casual chat, email or invite, calendar entry, school or office notice, community event listing, non-native English.
- Regions: US, UK, Canada, Australia, India, Pakistan, Bangladesh, Malaysia, Singapore, Indonesia, China, Hong Kong, Taiwan, Vietnam, Korea, Israel, the Gulf, and others. Use each region's names and habits.
- Combinations: a holiday with a time window, a holiday with a year, two holidays in one phrase, a holiday next to a weekday.
