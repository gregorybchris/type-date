# Relative weekends, offsets from now, parts of a month (round of 2026-11-21)

Goal: phrases a person types or writes that name a weekend relative to today, a time some hours or minutes from now, or a part of a month (its first or last weekend, week, or day). This file lists the kinds of phrasing we already expect. It shows no output formats. We want to find kinds we have not thought of, so unusual real phrasings matter more than many copies of the common ones.

## Families

|---|---|---|

## Variation to cover

- Context words around the phrase: "remind me", "can we meet", "book", "out of office", "deadline", "party", "trip", "standup", event names, filler ("pls", "maybe", "approx").
- Registers: terse quick-add, voice transcript (numbers as words, no punctuation), casual chat message, email or invite, calendar entry, forum post, event listing, non-native English.
- Regions: US, UK, Ireland, Canada, Australia, India, and others. Use each region's habits ("fortnight", "the weekend after this one", "this weekend just gone").
- Combinations: a relative weekend with a time window, a month part with a time, a span from now with a duration, a month name abbreviated or misspelled.
